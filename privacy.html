Privacy policy

Last updated November 15, 2026. Applies to the Unplugged browser extension, version 1.3.0 and later.

In short: Unplugged doesn't collect, send, sell or share any of your data. Everything it keeps stays in your browser on your own computer.

What Unplugged keeps, and where

Unplugged stores a few things with the browser's own extension storage (chrome.storage.local) on your computer, so it can do its job:

WhatWhyHow long
Your settings, including any change waiting on the 24-hour lockSo your choices stickUntil you change them or remove the extension
The streaming sites you add for episode limitsSo the limits run on those sitesUntil you remove them or the extension
Daily counts: YouTube and TikTok minutes watched, how many times you opened each site, time on episode pagesDaily limits and the "Today" numbers in the settingsDeleted after two weeks
The addresses of episode pages you started that daySo going back to an episode you already started doesn't count twiceDeleted after two weeks
Your risky-sites and never-block listsPop-up protection and blocking exceptionsUntil you remove them or the extension
The name and address of a program download it stoppedTo show the "download stopped" page and let you download it anywayOnly until you close the browser

None of this leaves your computer. Unplugged has no account, no servers, no analytics, no ads and no tracking. It doesn't read your messages, the content of pages you visit, or your browsing on other sites.

How blocking works

Adult and gambling sites are blocked by your browser using lists that come inside the extension (built from The Block List Project's public lists). Your browser checks each page against those lists itself; Unplugged doesn't see, keep or send the addresses you visit.

Where Unplugged runs

Unplugged runs on youtube.com, instagram.com and tiktok.com to hide feeds and show its pause and limit screens. It runs on any other site only after you add that site to your episode-limit or risky-sites list and approve it in your browser's own permission prompt. You can remove a site at any time.

Permissions it asks for

  • Storage: to keep the settings and counts described above on your computer.
  • Scripting: to run on the sites you add yourself (episode limits and risky sites), and nowhere else.
  • Block content on any page: to block adult and gambling sites from the built-in lists.
  • Manage your downloads: to stop program downloads and ask you first. Other downloads aren't touched.
  • Site access: YouTube, Instagram and TikTok when installed; other sites only one at a time, when you add them.

Removing your data

Removing the extension deletes everything it stored. If a feedback page opens when you remove it, filling it in is entirely up to you, and Unplugged sends nothing on its own.

Feedback and email

If you email us or send feedback, we only use what you write to reply and to improve Unplugged. We don't share it.

Changes
